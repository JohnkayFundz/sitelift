Website Audit Checklist for Small Businesses

Your website should help customers understand what you offer, trust your business, and take the next step. Use this checklist to review the basics. You can also use SiteLift's free audit and 50-point manual checklist to support a more structured review.

1. First impressions and trust

  • Can a new visitor tell what the business does within a few seconds?
  • Are the business name, services or products, and service area clear?
  • Are prices shown where appropriate, or is requesting a quote straightforward?
  • Are contact details, opening hours, and policies easy to find?
  • Are images current, relevant, and good quality?
  • Are testimonials and claims genuine and accurately represented?

2. Mobile experience

  • Does the page fit a phone screen without horizontal scrolling?
  • Can visitors read text without zooming?
  • Are buttons and navigation links easy to tap?
  • Do menus, forms, and product galleries work on a phone?
  • Can customers find the main action, such as calling, booking, or asking a question?

3. Speed and stability

  • Are images resized and compressed for their display size?
  • Does the main content appear promptly on a typical mobile connection?
  • Do layout elements jump around while the page loads?
  • Are unnecessary widgets, trackers, or scripts slowing down the page?
  • Do important pages and images load without errors?

Use the website speed test guide to understand performance measurements. A single lab score is not a guarantee of the experience every visitor will have.

4. Search basics

  • Does every important page have a unique, descriptive title?
  • Does the main heading accurately describe the page?
  • Are descriptions and page content useful rather than repetitive?
  • Do internal links lead to the correct destinations?
  • Are the sitemap and robots.txt files accurate for the site?
  • Can you inspect search indexing in Google Search Console?

For more detail, read the beginner's website SEO guide.

5. Contact and enquiries

  • Do phone, email, booking, and WhatsApp links open the correct destination?
  • Does each form explain what information is required?
  • Does the visitor receive clear confirmation after submitting a form?
  • Do enquiries reach a person who can respond?
  • Is the next step obvious without being pushy?
  • Is personal information handled responsibly, with privacy information where appropriate?

How to use the checklist

Review the website on a phone and a desktop if possible. Record each issue with the page URL, what you observed, why it matters to customers, and the suggested next step. Fix critical broken journeys first, then address high-impact usability and performance problems. Re-test after making changes.

Keep findings honest.

Do not tell a business that its website is losing sales or ranking poorly unless you have evidence. Describe what you actually observed and explain what could be tested or improved.
